01

Triage deck — the list is the product

Today the list is a uniform 52 px wall: six titles, six timestamps, no order. This turns it into a queue with one loud row and three quiet buckets, so the first decision is made before you read a diff. The detail pane stops opening on the conversation and opens on the pre-flight: why you, where the risk is, what it costs.

  • One hero per screen. The single row that needs an answer is taller, lit, and carries the action that ends it.
  • Buckets, not filters. Blocked on you / Waiting on author / Drafts — state decides order, not recency.
  • Cost before commitment. Files, semantic steps, last verdict and estimated minutes sit next to the CTA.
  • Context before code. The Linear issue fills a fixed slot — state, owner, acceptance. Unlinked PRs fall back to the description, so the pane never blanks.

Review mode — a cockpit, not a report

The review tab today reads like a generated document: verdict, then a long column of cards. Review mode makes it a workflow. Findings become a spine you step through, the evidence sits beside them, and the decision is always one keystroke away. Chrome collapses so the only things on screen are the next finding and the submit.

  • Progress is visible. Two blocking findings, one decided; the pips and the spine states agree.
  • Evidence, then action. Prose → hunk → apply — all in the center column, never a modal.
  • The submit is a panel. Verdict choice and note live next to the finding, so you can finish without hunting a toolbar.

03

Anchor spine — one object, three surfaces

A comment, a finding and a diff line are the same fact seen from three sides. Right now they are three disconnected lists. Here every anchor carries the same id: click a comment and the line, the finding chip and the semantic pin light up together. The gutter advertises what depends on a line before you read it.

  • Click any anchor in the frame (a comment, a line, a pin) to see the link highlight in all three surfaces.
  • The gutter is informative. A 6 px mark means "a finding or comment is attached here", not decoration.
  • The semantic rail earns its width. Ticks keep the shape; pins mark where findings actually land.
  • The rail is the map. Files in one mode, the numbered review path in the other; the active step pins above its hunks.

04

State contract — one recipe per state, no synonyms

The three stories above argue composition; they leave the states implicit. This sheet makes them explicit. Pixels first, then the rule: every highlight, cursor, selection, disabled and linked surface has exactly one class, one selector and one recipe. If a state is not in this sheet, it is not part of the direction.

  • Two tiers of highlight. is-active is the keyboard cursor and follows the eye; is-selected is the committed pick and survives a refetch. A row is never both.
  • One selection recipe. Deck rows and repo rows share the shipped row-selected utility from src/index.css: a left-entering gradient plus a 2px accent bar with a halo. Severity bars lose to it.
  • The suffix is the meaning. is-on toggle · is-active current item · is-selected committed · is-link linked anchor · is-done decided · is-dim de-emphasised.
  • Hover is shown, not felt. Gallery cells replay the real :hover recipe statically via .spec-hover; the product still uses the pseudo-class.

Deck row six states, one axis

Same row in every cell so only the state changes. Severity is sev-blocked except the dim cell. The bar is the severity channel; selection replaces it with the accent channel.

Resolved on canvas #09090b: cursor #0e0e10 (surface 60%) · hover #0f0f11 (surface 70%) · selected starts at #111113 on the left edge and fades to canvas by 82%. Severity bar: 2px at rest, 3px on hover, replaced by the 2px accent bar when selected.

Diff line one left edge, four meanings

All annotation is a 2px inset edge on the left. Accent means "linked right now"; danger means "finding attached"; neutral means "pointer". Linked wins over marked; add/del tint is the background, not the edge.
